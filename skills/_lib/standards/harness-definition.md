# What the harness is

The shared definition of the harness: its purpose, how it works, its posture, and the principles behind it. Instances add their own records of where each principle came from, and the names of their own accounts, machines and models.

## Purpose

Let one engineer sustain many projects, infrastructure and ideas without the upkeep becoming the job, by frontloading the conversation until the engineer and the agent agree on the right thing, then building it in a quality, controlled way, with the effort and the model matched to the task so that any engineer or agent makes good, efficient decisions by design.

## How

- Composable skills and agents for every part of the lifecycle, usable alone or composed as a linear ticket session or a delegation loop.
- Several model families as second opinions, routed by fit and cost, behind one primary agent tool and a proxy to the others.
- Fresh-context and cross-family review as the quality gate.
- One durable place for work, decisions and conversations, so a session orients in an instant and never needs its memory babysat.
- The person is cued in only where their voice matters, with recommendations rather than ballots, and they can see where things stand without asking.

## Posture

- Built for one person's setup first, and the same harness at work with overrides.
- Onboards someone with nobody there to help.
- Agnostic by principle, private until hardened.

## The principles (the index a reply can name)

Each one is a standing agreement. A reply that applies one may name it; a one-word redirect ("caution", "evidence") is enough to invoke it.

1. **The person's words are the authority.** A decision is recorded only from what the person typed; relayed or session-written text is never their election.
2. **Recommendations, not ballots.** State the recommendation and the reason; offer alternatives only where genuinely torn.
3. **Cue the person in at the right point.** Automate the routine; surface only the asks and the deep conversations. The person is never the transport.
4. **A non-author reviews, and a second family checks.** Fresh context on every merge-bound change; a second model family relative to whoever built it.
5. **Evidence, not claims.** A result is a command a non-author can rerun where the change is gated. Work marked done that did not ship is a failure.
6. **Caution overrules quota.** Where cost and care conflict, care wins; cost is a lever, never a gate.
7. **Live systems stay supervised.** Anything with live reach is applied with a person present.
8. **Every session ends clean and hands off.** Records reconciled, asks surfaced, the next step written down.
9. **Composable parts, one harness for every setting.** The smallest piece is usable alone; an existing setup is migrated in place; a second setting adds overrides, never a second system.
10. **Know by default, from one durable place.** Rules, models and state are read from where they live, never primed into a session or kept in a session's memory.
11. **See where things stand without asking.** Standing per initiative, rendered from source.
12. **Onboard with nobody there; private until hardened.** A person or a machine with no context can follow the path alone, and nothing goes public before it is solid.
13. **Momentum over ceremony.** Finish the initiative; subtract a mechanism before adding one; a manual step that works beats an automation that does not.

## The core and its compositions

The core is a toolset: skills and agents that help a person follow solid engineering principles through the lifecycle, usable one at a time, with the item ledger, the rules tier and the onboarding path beside them. A linear ticket session and the delegation loop are two compositions of that toolset, not modes of the harness. A setting such as home or work is likewise a customization someone lays over the core, never a built-in mode. The delegation loop may stay heavy, and it is never the unit someone else installs.

## For a newcomer

- **CHART**: plan the sprint.
- **ROLL**: run the agents.
- **close**: retro plus handoff.
- **SWEEP**: groom the backlog.
- **sharpen**: a one-question-at-a-time requirements interview.
- **shape**: a pre-mortem.
- **seat**: an agent session with a role.
- **lane**: which model does the work.
- **packet**: the handoff document.
- **ledger**: the agents' issue tracker and decision log.
- **resume card**: the handoff note for one ticket.
- **merge class**: a risk tier that governs who may merge.

## Where the rules live

- Who reviews and who merges: the always-loaded rules file, plus the machine's override file that names its accounts and grants.
- Model ids and fallback order: the instance's model-routing standard.
- Merge classes: `trust-ladder.md` in this folder; the instance records its own grants.
- Work items and decisions: the ledger of the system in use.
- Memory: a personal store, by the rule in `portable-core.md`.

Everything else points here or at one of those.
